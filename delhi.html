About New Delhi

New Delhi is the capital territory of India, a sprawling megalopolis of over 32 million people that seamlessly blends ancient heritage with futuristic modernity. Serving as the seat of the Indian government since 1911, this dynamic city occupies the heart of the Indo-Gangetic plain along the banks of the Yamuna River.

Delhi's history stretches back over 5,000 years — it has been the capital of successive empires including the Mauryas, Tughlaqs, Mughals, and the British Raj. This rich layered past means the city is a living museum, where 17th-century Mughal forts, British Raj-era boulevards, and gleaming 21st-century malls all coexist within minutes of each other.

The city is broadly divided into Old Delhi — the medieval walled city with its labyrinthine lanes, bazaars, and Mughal monuments — and New Delhi, the planned colonial capital designed by Edwin Lutyens with wide tree-lined avenues, grand government buildings, and diplomatic enclaves. Together they form one of the world's most historically layered and culturally vibrant urban landscapes.

As India's gastronomic capital, Delhi is equally celebrated for its food culture — from the legendary street food lanes of Chandni Chowk serving chole bhature, parathas, and kebabs, to the fine dining restaurants of Connaught Place dishing out global cuisine. Delhi truly offers something extraordinary for every traveller.

Must-See Attractions

🏯

Red Fort (Lal Qila)

UNESCO World Heritage Site. Built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in 1638, this red sandstone citadel was the main residence of Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years. Explore its stunning halls, museums, and the iconic Lahori Gate.

🏛️

India Gate

A 42-metre war memorial dedicated to 84,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army. The eternal Amar Jawan Jyoti flame burns beneath its arch. The surrounding Rajpath boulevard is the venue for Republic Day parades.

🕌

Jama Masjid

India's largest mosque, built by Shah Jahan between 1644–1656. The mosque can hold up to 25,000 worshippers and offers panoramic views of Old Delhi from its minarets. A masterpiece of Mughal architecture.

⚰️

Humayun's Tomb

The magnificent mausoleum of the second Mughal Emperor Humayun, built in 1570. Considered the forerunner of the Taj Mahal, this UNESCO site pioneered the concept of the "charbagh" — formal Persian garden design.

🛕

Akshardham Temple

A breathtaking modern Hindu temple complex spanning 100 acres. Features intricate stone carvings, boat rides, a musical fountain show, and exhibitions on Indian culture and spirituality. A true architectural marvel.

⚙️

Qutub Minar

The world's tallest brick minaret at 72.5 metres, built in 1193 CE. This UNESCO World Heritage Site is one of the earliest examples of Afghan architecture in India and is surrounded by medieval ruins and the mysterious Iron Pillar.

🛍️

Chandni Chowk

One of the oldest and busiest markets in India. A sensory overload of colour, aroma, and flavour. Explore spice markets, textile shops, jewellery bazaars, and taste some of the world's best street food all in one lane.

🌸

Lodhi Garden

A 90-acre public park dotted with 15th-century Lodi dynasty tombs amid manicured gardens. A peaceful urban escape beloved by Delhiites for morning walks, jogging, and weekend picnics amidst Mughal-era architecture.

Travel Tips

  • Use the Delhi Metro: It's the fastest, cheapest, and most comfortable way to get around. Covers all major tourist spots. A tourist day pass costs just ₹200.

  • Stay Safe: Keep your belongings secure in crowded markets. Use authorised prepaid taxis or Ola/Uber for rides. Avoid unmarked autos late at night.

  • Stay Hydrated: Delhi summers are brutal. Carry a reusable water bottle and drink only sealed/filtered water. Tender coconut water is your best friend.

  • Dress Modestly: When visiting mosques and temples, cover your shoulders and knees. Remove footwear before entering religious sites.

  • Bargain at Markets: At local bazaars like Chandni Chowk and Sarojini Nagar, always negotiate. Starting price is often 2–3x the fair value.

  • Air Quality: Delhi's AQI can be poor Nov–Jan due to crop burning. Consider an N95 mask. Check AQI on apps like IQAir before outdoor excursions.
